People Operations built from zero

Ridecell | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

Ridecell had no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in spreadsheets, and there was no tracked channel for employee questions.

Result

Supported the company across 3 to 5+ countries, 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, approval cycles cut from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and 5-10 tickets a day resolved within 24-48 hours.

What I did, and what I would do differently

What I did

Built a 40+ procedure SOP library with named owners and backup coverage, ASKPeople (a Jira and Confluence ticketing system with tiered SLAs), and Sapling as the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected through Zapier.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

    My first 90 days in a Director of People Operations role

    A starting point I would adapt after the first conversations with leadership and the team.

    Days 1 to 30

    Listen and map

    • Meet the executive team, the People team, and partners in Finance, Legal, and IT, and learn what is working and what hurts.
    • Audit the foundation: HRIS data quality, payroll accuracy, policies, the compliance calendar, and open employee cases.
    • Map the employee lifecycle end to end, with an owner and a handoff for every step.
    Days 31 to 60

    Design the first moves

    • Fix the highest-risk gaps first: data accuracy, approvals, and compliance deadlines.
    • Document the core workflows and stand up one tracked channel for employee questions.
    • Decide where automation helps and where human judgment has to stay.
    Days 61 to 90

    Ship and measure

    • Launch one automation on a real workflow, with a human review step.
    • Report turnaround time, data accuracy, and compliance status before and after.
    • Share a 12-month People Operations roadmap with leadership, built from what I learned.
    By month 6

    The outcome

    • Policies, systems, and data are documented, current, and audit-ready.
    • Managers and employees know where to go and get answers quickly.
    • More of the team's week goes to conversations and judgment calls, and less to manual work.

    Every company is different, so I would adjust this plan after listening to the executive team, the People team, and my partners in Finance, Legal, and IT.

    Questions I get asked

    Quick answers

    Have you run a reduction in force?

    Yes. At Prezent AI I planned and ran one across the US and India for 18 employees, with severance, COBRA, and agreements modeled and drafted using AI, plus a shared tracker for Payroll and IT. At Munchery I managed a WARN Act closure affecting close to 100 employees. At Ridecell I supported the company through multiple reductions and restructurings with no compliance violations.

    Which HRIS and payroll systems have you used?

    Rippling, ADP Workforce Now, Kallidus Sapling, Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, UKG, PeopleSoft, and FinancialForce HCM. I led the Sapling implementation and integrated it with ADP, which cut approval turnaround from 1 to 2 days to 2 to 3 hours.

    How do you use AI in People Ops?

    For the routine work, with a human reviewing anything that needs judgment. I built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that reached 100% adoption and created an audit trail that did not exist before. I also built severance and cost models, and automations with Zapier and n8n.

    How do you handle compliance in more than one country?

    I build the program once and adapt it by jurisdiction: I-9, FLSA, GDPR, and CCPA, plus Employer of Record setups in Canada, the UK, and Dubai. I have partnered with five outside immigration counsels, and the audit record is six clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits in a row.

    Have you only worked at startups?

    No. I started at large corporations: Quiksilver/Roxy/DC, VF Corporation (Lucy and The North Face), and Dolby. That is where I learned process, compliance, and scale. I then moved to startups on purpose, to grow faster and own more, first at Munchery and then at Ridecell, where I built the People function and kept 90% of the employees I took on during the 2019 scaling for 5+ years.

    Why did you leave Prezent AI?

    The team was in India and I was in the US, and the time zone gap made the role unsustainable for me long term. I am looking for a role where my hours overlap with my team and leaders, EST to PST. At Prezent I still ran a 60-day audit, an acquisition integration, and a reorganization, and I led a team of 8 that I restructured to 6 and grew back to 7.

    What size team have you led?

    At Ridecell, 2 direct reports plus 2 dotted-line recruiters, while owning People Operations for 350+ employees. At Prezent AI, an India-based team of 8, restructured to 6 and then 7.

    Are you more strategic or hands-on?

    Both, and I think a first People leader has to be. I wrote the SOP library, and I also personally onboarded and offboarded every Ridecell employee.

    What would your first 30 days look like?

    Listening and mapping. I would meet the executive team, the People team, and partners in Finance, Legal, and IT to learn what is working, then document the current state before changing anything. The full 30-60-90 plan is further down the page.

    How do you work with managers?

    I give them tools and early involvement rather than rules after the fact. That meant a manager toolkit, early HR involvement before any termination, and an offboarding process that moved our Glassdoor rating from 3.6 to 3.9.
